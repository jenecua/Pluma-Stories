Portrait on the Wall

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: The Crime Scene


        The crime scene lay within a grand mansion in the city, its vast halls and corridors stretching in every direction. Police officers and Crime Scene Investigators moved methodically, examining every
        
        corner with precision.

        Earlier that morning, a dead body had been discovered in the garden by the maid, Lina. The gardener was immediately arrested. Detective Jonathan Pierce arrived to interview the owner of the house,
        
        Ms. Emma Montreal. Emma, a woman in her forties, was sophisticated, intelligent, and strikingly beautiful. She was also the wealthiest resident of Main City.
        
        The victim was her butler, Gregory, a kind-hearted young man in his twenties, beloved by the maids and other employees. No one could imagine who might have harbored anger toward him.
        
        He had been stabbed repeatedly until his final breath.

        “Ms. Montreal, we’re sorry about what happened,” Detective Pierce said gently. “You don’t have to worry. We will investigate thoroughly to find the suspect and bring them behind bars.”
        
        Emma simply nodded. “Do you have any more questions? If you’ll excuse me, I want to rest,” she said.
        
        “Of course, Ms. Montreal. I apologize for the inconvenience,” the detective replied. Emma turned away and walked back inside the mansion.
        
        As Pierce headed toward his patrol car, he overheard the maids whispering. They speculated that the gardener had killed the butler because Gregory had become Emma’s favorite.
        
        The detective approached them sternly. “Do not spread rumors. Lies can put you in jail,” he warned. Frightened, the maids hurried inside the mansion.
        
        Pierce reflected on Emma’s words and her demeanor during the interview. Unlike the other employees, she had been serious, reserved, almost unwilling to be questioned.
        
        He studied the notes he had taken and reviewed the photographs captured by the investigators. Something about her expression lingered in his mind.
        
        Finally, he walked to his car, started the engine, and drove back to the police station, the weight of unanswered questions pressing heavily on his thoughts.

        As he pulled away from the curb, a detail in one of the photographs suddenly caught his attention. Standing in an upstairs window of the mansion was a faint silhouette, watching the investigation
        
        unfold. Yet according to the staff records, that room had been locked and unoccupied for years.
