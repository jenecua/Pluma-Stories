Golden Plate

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: The Dog in the Moonlight


        The cold wind swept through the sleeping city as an old beggar wandered the empty streets. His name was Tomas. For years, he had lived with nothing but the clothes on his back and a worn blanket he carried
        
        wherever he went. His hair was gray, his face was lined with wrinkles, and his body had become thin from countless days of hunger. Tonight was no different. His stomach growled loudly as he sat beneath a
        
        flickering lantern.
        
        Three days. Three whole days had passed since he had eaten a proper meal. Tomas looked up at the bright moon hanging in the sky. "If only I could have a piece of bread," he whispered.
        
        The city offered no answer. Only silence. Then suddenly, something moved in the darkness. A dog emerged from a nearby alley. Tomas narrowed his eyes. The animal wasn't carrying a bone or a scrap of food.
        
        It was carrying an entire roasted chicken. The old man's eyes widened. A whole chicken. Golden brown and glistening with juices. The delicious aroma drifted through the cold air. Tomas swallowed hard.
        
        His empty stomach seemed to scream. "Where did that dog get such food?" he wondered aloud. The dog glanced back at him. For a moment, their eyes met. Then it continued walking.
        
        Tomas rose to his feet. "Forgive me," he muttered. "But I have to know." Driven by hunger, he followed. The dog trotted through narrow alleys and deserted streets. Every now and then it glanced back as if
        
        checking whether Tomas was still behind it. The chase continued for several minutes. Soon they arrived at an old stone wall hidden behind abandoned buildings. Without hesitation, the dog slipped through a
        
        hole near its base.
        
        Tomas hesitated. He had never seen this place before. The wall seemed ancient, almost forgotten by time. Curiosity overcame caution. He crouched down and crawled through the opening.
        
        The moment he reached the other side, he froze. Before him stretched a hidden garden bathed in silver moonlight. Flowers of blue, purple, and gold glowed softly in the darkness. Tiny lights floated through
        
        the air like fireflies made of stars. At the center stood a small wooden house. Warm light spilled from its windows. The dog ran straight toward it. Tomas followed. His heart pounded in his chest.
        
        Nothing about this place felt real. When he reached the front door, he noticed it was already slightly open. Slowly, he pushed it wider. The room inside was empty.
        
        No owner.
        
        No family.
        
        No sound except the crackling of a fireplace.
        
        The dog jumped onto a table standing in the center of the room. There was something resting upon it. A golden plate. It shone so brightly that it seemed to have captured moonlight itself.
        
        The dog nudged the plate toward him with its nose. Tomas blinked. Then the impossible happened.
        
        "Take it."
        
        The old man nearly fell backward. His eyes widened in terror. The dog had spoken.
        
        "You... you can talk?"
        
        The dog wagged its tail.
        
        "Of course."
        
        Tomas stared in complete disbelief. Had hunger finally driven him mad. Was he dreaming? The dog pushed the plate closer.
        
        "Don't be afraid. Take the plate."
        
        Slowly, Tomas reached for it.
        
        The metal felt warm beneath his trembling fingers.
        
        "What is this?" he asked.
        
        "A gift," said the dog.
        
        "A gift?"
        
        The dog nodded.

        "Wish for anything you desire, and the plate will grant it."
        
        Tomas frowned. That sounded impossible. Magic existed only in stories. Yet tonight he had already followed a dog carrying a whole chicken into a hidden house beyond an ancient wall.
        
        Perhaps impossible things were possible after all. The dog sat patiently.
        
        "Go on," it said.
        
        Tomas looked down at the plate. His stomach growled loudly. If this was a joke, he had nothing to lose. Closing his eyes, he took a deep breath.
        
        "I wish for ham and fresh bread."
        
        For a moment, nothing happened. Then golden light spread across the surface of the plate. The room began to glow. Tomas gasped.
        
        The light swirled and danced like liquid sunshine. A second later, a large slice of glazed ham appeared on the plate. Beside it rested a warm loaf of bread. Steam rose from both.
        
        The delicious scent filled the room instantly. Tomas stood frozen. His hands shook. "It can't be..." He reached out and touched the bread. It was real. Warm. Fresh.
        
        Carefully, he tore off a piece and placed it in his mouth. The soft texture melted against his tongue. Never in his life had he tasted bread so wonderful. Tears filled his eyes.
        
        Then he tasted the ham. The flavorful meat was rich and tender. His body trembled. After years of begging. After years of hunger. After countless nights sleeping on cold streets.
        
        He was finally eating a proper meal. A tear rolled down his cheek. Then another. And another. The dog watched quietly.
        
        "Why are you crying?" it asked. Tomas looked at the magical plate before him. "I don't know," he said softly. His voice cracked.
        
        "Maybe because for the first time in a very long time... I have hope." The dog smiled.
        
        Or at least, Tomas thought it smiled. Outside, the moon continued to shine over the hidden garden. And deep within the mysterious house, fate had just changed the life of a forgotten old man.
        
        Little did Tomas know, this magical plate was only the beginning.
        
        Something far greater awaited him.
