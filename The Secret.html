The Secret

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: The Fear


        All the while, the secret gnawed at her. It haunted her nights, stealing sleep from her eyes, and hollowed her days, leaving food tasteless on her tongue. “Why must I see it?” she whispered to herself,
        
        over and over.

        Anne woke to another ordinary morning, though nothing felt ordinary anymore. She rose from her bed, the weight of hunger pressing against her ribs, and prepared to forage.
        
        When she opened her front door, the dawn air rushed in—cool, sharp, alive. Birds sang in the distance, trees swayed like dancers in the half-light, and the sun lingered just below the horizon, waiting.
        
        She pulled on her worn boots, strapped her apron, and tucked her pocket knife into her hand. With steady steps she made her way to the foot of the hill, searching every patch of earth for berries.
        
        An hour passed before a sound shattered the silence—a shout, raw and sudden. Panic seized her chest. Everyone knew the rule: the woods were forbidden.
        
        The elders had warned them countless times—those who entered never returned. No one knew why.
        
        Curiosity burned inside her, but fear kept her rooted. She turned back, clutching her basket, and returned home. She laid the berries on her table, sat down, and let her thoughts spiral.
        
        “What lies within the woods? Is there truly something to fear? Why has the sheriff done nothing?” The questions circled endlessly, like vultures above carrion.

        Her stomach growled, breaking the spell. She spread wild berry jam over bread and butter, but even as she ate, her mind wandered back to the forest. Night fell, and still she thought of the shout.
        
        “I want to know what’s in there. I truly do.” Sleep finally claimed her, though her dreams were restless.

        At dawn… Anne rose with determination. She dressed in black—shirt, pants, shoes—like a shadow preparing to slip unseen. Knife in hand, she crept toward the woods, careful that no eyes would follow.
        
        The forest swallowed her whole. Mist clung to the ground, and silence pressed against her ears. Soon she found a hut, small and crooked, not far from the entrance.
        
        She edged closer, but footsteps echoed from within. Heart pounding, she dove into the bushes, peering through the leaves.
        
        The door creaked open. The sheriff stepped out. Relief washed over her, a fragile breath escaping her lips. But then another figure emerged—a man, broad-shouldered,
        
        bare-chested, his skin smeared with blood. He carried an axe in his right hand, its blade glinting in the pale light.
        
        Anne froze. Her legs turned to stone, her body numb, her heart racing like a trapped bird. Terror rooted her in place.

        The man disappeared back into the hut, and the sheriff walked away. Only then did Anne crawl, inch by inch, until the village path appeared before her.
        
        She staggered to her feet and ran, branches clawing at her arms, until she reached her home.
        
        She slammed the door shut, bolted the windows, and collapsed against the wood. Her breath came in ragged gasps. Her mind circled back to the vision—the sheriff,
        
        the bloodied man, the axe gleaming in the dawn.
        
        And she knew: the secret would never let her go.
