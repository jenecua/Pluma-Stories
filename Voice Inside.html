Voice Inside

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: Father's Secret

        
        Gary Morrison had been afraid of his father for as long as he could remember.
        
        Not because of the shouting. Not because of the beatings. But because of what he had seen when he was seven years old. It was a rainy night.
        
        Gary had been unable to sleep and wandered downstairs in search of a glass of water. That was when he saw it. His father standing in the garage.
        
        A man was lying on the floor. Bleeding. Begging. Gary could still remember every detail even after all these years. The trembling hands. The terrified eyes. The sound of his father's voice. And the knife.
        
        Again. And again. And again. The next morning, his father sat beside him at the breakfast table as though nothing had happened.
        
        Gary had barely touched his food. His father leaned closer and smiled.
        
        It was the most frightening smile Gary had ever seen. "If you tell anyone what you saw," his father whispered, "I'll kill you too."
        
        From that day forward, fear became a permanent part of Gary's life. He never told the police.
        
        He never told his teachers. He never told his friends. Instead, he buried the memory deep within himself and pretended everything was normal.
        
        Years passed.
        
        Then one day, when Gary was twenty-five years old, his father died of a heart attack. The funeral was small. Few people came.
        
        Even fewer cried. As Gary stood beside the grave, he felt something he hadn't experienced since childhood.
        
        Relief. Finally, it was over. The nightmares. The fear. The threats. His father was gone. At least, that's what he believed.
        
        Three months later, Gary was walking home from work when he heard a familiar voice. "Look at that man." Gary froze.
        
        The voice sounded exactly like his father's. He looked around. The sidewalk was crowded, but nobody seemed to notice.
        
        "The one in the black jacket." Gary's heartbeat accelerated. "Kill him." "No," Gary muttered.
        
        People walking nearby glanced at him strangely. "Kill him." Gary covered his ears. The voice only grew louder. "Do it."
        
        He hurried home. That night, he barely slept. The voice returned. Again. And again. And again. Each day it became stronger.
        
        More demanding. More aggressive. The voice would appear while he ate. While he worked. While he tried to sleep. Always asking for the same thing. Kill. Gary didn't understand.
        
        He was nothing like his father. He had never hurt anyone.
        
        In fact, everyone described him as kind and gentle. Yet the voice refused to leave. Days turned into weeks. Weeks turned into months.
        
        Then one evening, Gary sat alone in his apartment. Rain tapped against the windows. The room was silent. For once, the voice had stopped.
        
        Gary sighed with relief. Perhaps it was finally over. Then he heard it. A whisper. So faint he almost missed it. "Good boy."
        
        Gary's blood turned cold. The voice chuckled. "You've been listening." "I don't want this," Gary whispered. "You don't have a choice." "Leave me alone."
        
        "Or what?" Gary squeezed his eyes shut. The voice laughed. It sounded exactly like his father. "You know what happens if you disobey me."
        
        Gary's hands trembled. His father was dead. He knew that. He had buried him himself.
        
        Yet somehow, every night, the man who haunted his childhood seemed to be sitting right beside him. Watching. Waiting. Smiling.
        
        And deep inside, Gary feared something far worse than insanity. Because part of him was beginning to listen.
