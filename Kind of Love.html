Kind of Love

Author: R.J.C

Chapter 1: The Discovery

        
        Kyle’s sobs echoed through the quiet house. His chest heaved, his eyes swollen from crying. He didn’t know what to do now that his father had discovered his relationship with Drew.
        
        Richard had always loved him deeply, had dreamed of seeing his son married and raising a family of his own. But now, after witnessing with his own eyes that Kyle was in love with
        
        another man, his heart had clenched in disbelief. The air between them had turned heavy—filled with silence, pain, and questions neither could answer.

        Five Months Earlier.
        
        The plane touched down in Montmayor, its wheels screeching against the runway. Kyle stepped out into the humid air, dragging his suitcase behind him. After ten long years, he was finally home.
        
        Outside the airport, he hailed a taxi and watched the city blur past the window—billboards, cars, and the faint scent of rain on concrete.

        When he reached his father’s house, he knocked softly. The door opened, and Richard stood there, older but smiling. The longing in his eyes was unmistakable.
        
        “Welcome home, son,” he said, his voice trembling.

        Kyle dropped his luggage and hugged him tightly. For a moment, the years apart melted away.

        His parents’ divorce had torn their family in two. His mother had remarried, and her new husband refused to live with a child who wasn’t his own. Kyle hadn’t argued when she told him he would move
        back to his father’s care. He knew Richard would love him unconditionally.

        Three Days Later.
        
        Richard had already enrolled Kyle in a private university. He was proud of his son, eager to give him the best education possible.
        
        Richard was a civil engineer—hardworking, disciplined, and devoted. His marriage had fallen apart because of that devotion. His wife had wanted affection and time; Richard had
        
        wanted stability and security. In the end, love had lost to responsibility.

        The First Day of Class.
        
        The alarm blared at six in the morning. Kyle groaned, rubbing his eyes. He was already two weeks late for the semester, and anxiety gnawed at him.
        
        He dressed quickly, grabbed his bag, and hurried out the door.
        
        At the university, he found his classroom and slipped into a vacant chair along the aisle. The chatter of students filled the room, but he kept his head down, trying not to
        
        draw attention.
            
        Two boys approached him, smirking.
        
        “Hey, new guy,” one said. “Got any cash?”
            
        Before Kyle could respond, they snatched his bag and rummaged through it. His knuckles whitened as he clenched his fists, humiliated. After taking what they wanted,
        
        the bullies tossed his bag back carelessly and sauntered off, laughing.
            
        Kyle exhaled shakily, trying to compose himself.
            
        A girl nearby stood up after the bullies left. Kyle looked at her, hopeful, and managed a small smile. But her smile was sharp, dripping with sarcasm.
        
        “You look smart,” she said, then added, “but don’t think you’ll ever beat me in grades.”
            
        The words cut deeper than he expected. His smile faltered, the warmth in his eyes fading into quiet disappointment. He lowered his gaze, wishing he could disappear.
            
        Across the row, Drew had been watching quietly. He had seen the bullies, heard the girl’s biting remark, and noticed the way Kyle’s expression shifted from hope to hurt.
        
        Drew’s eyes lingered on him,
        
        filled with a sympathy he didn’t dare show.
            
        Kyle glanced at him, searching for some sign of kindness. But Drew quickly turned away, pretending not to notice, though inside he felt a pull he couldn’t explain.
