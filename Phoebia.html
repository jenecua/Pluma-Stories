Phoebia

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: Blood

        
        Ethan Hart hated blood. Not disliked. But feared.
        
        The sight of it made his stomach twist and his lungs lock. Even a tiny drop was enough to send panic rushing through his body. When he was ten years old, he witnessed a terrible accident.
        
        A memory he could never forget. A memory that followed him into every nightmare. Since then, blood had become his prison. At twenty-eight years old, Ethan had not stepped outside his house
        
        in nearly three years.
        
        The world beyond his front door terrified him. A car accident. A fight. A person falling on the sidewalk. Any of those things could result in blood. And blood meant panic.
        
        His house was the only place where he felt safe. Every delivery was left outside. Every bill was paid online. Every conversation happened through a screen. The outside world no longer existed
        
        to him.
        
        And he preferred it that way. At exactly eight in the morning, Ethan sat at his kitchen table, eating cereal while staring out the window. Nothing unusual.
        
        The same empty street. The same silent neighborhood. The same routine. Then something changed. A red stain appeared on the sidewalk across the street. Ethan frowned.
        
        The stain hadn't been there yesterday. He stood and walked closer to the window. The stain looked wet. Fresh. His throat tightened. Blood. His hands started shaking.
        
        "No..." He stepped backward. There was no reason for blood to be there. No police cars. No ambulance. No disturbance. Just a dark red stain sitting alone on the pavement.
        
        A few minutes later, a woman walked directly over it. She didn't react. She continued walking as if nothing was there. Ethan blinked. Then a jogger passed over the stain. Again. No reaction.
        
        His breathing became uneven. Was he the only one seeing it? He grabbed a pair of binoculars from the shelf and looked through them. The stain was gone. Ethan lowered the binoculars.
        
        His heart pounded.
        
        He looked again. The stain had returned. Gone. Returned. Gone. He nearly dropped the binoculars. "What the hell is happening?" The rest of the day passed in silence. Ethan tried convincing himself
        
        it was stress.
        
        A trick of the mind. Nothing more. That night, he locked every door and checked every window twice. Then three times. He eventually fell asleep shortly after midnight.
        
        At 3:17 a.m., he woke to a loud knock. His eyes shot open. Knock. Knock. Knock. Someone was at the front door. Ethan remained frozen beneath the blanket. Nobody visited him.
        
        Nobody even knew where he lived. The knocking came again. Slow. Patient. Almost deliberate. His phone lit up on the nightstand. A text message. Unknown Number. With trembling fingers, Ethan opened it.
        
        A single photograph appeared on the screen. His front door. Taken from the outside. His breath caught in his throat. The image had been taken only seconds ago. Then another message arrived.
        
        This one contained only four words. I know what you saw. The blood drained from Ethan's face. A third message appeared. Look outside. Ethan slowly turned toward the bedroom window.
        
        Across the street stood a figure. Perfectly still. Watching his house. Watching him. And beneath the streetlight was a dark red stain that seemed larger than before.
        
        Much larger. As if it had never stopped growing.
