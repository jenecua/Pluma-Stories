Freedom

Author: R.J.C.

Chapter 1: Buried Freedom


        The first thing Liam Cruz learned was that freedom had a price. He was eight years old when he was taken.

        One moment, he was selling bottled water near a crowded bus terminal to help his sick mother. The next, he found himself in the back of a van with a sack over his head. No one came looking for him.
        
        No one could. By the time the police report was filed, Liam was already hundreds of miles away. For twelve years, he belonged to a gang
        
        known as the Black Vipers.

        They trained children to become thieves, smugglers, and enforcers. Some obeyed willingly. Others obeyed out of fear. Liam obeyed because he wanted to survive. But unlike the others,
        
        he never lost himself.
        
        He secretly shared food with younger recruits. He hid money for those planning to escape. Whenever he could, he protected the innocent from the gang's cruelty.
        
        That kindness made him dangerous. Because kind people remembered they had a conscience. And conscience could become rebellion.

        At twenty years old, Liam finally had enough.
        
        The gang boss, Ramon Velez, ruled through fear. Dozens had died because of him. Hundreds had suffered. One stormy night, Liam ended it. No witnesses.

        No celebration. No revenge. Only silence. When it was over, Liam buried Ramon deep within an abandoned forest where no one would ever find him. Or so he thought.

        The gang collapsed within weeks. Many captives escaped and started new lives. Liam disappeared among them. For the first time in twelve years, he felt free.

        Three months later, a hiker entered the forest. The smell reached him before the sight did. Minutes later, police sealed off the area. Detective Elena Reyes stood beside the shallow grave.

        "What do we know?" she asked. "A male victim," one officer replied. "Approximately fifty years old. Looks like he's been here for months." Elena examined the remains.

        Then she noticed something unusual. Wrapped around the victim's wrist was a small metal bracelet. On it were two engraved letters. R.V.

        The detective's eyes narrowed. She had seen those initials before. Years ago. In reports connected to missing children. A cold feeling settled in her stomach.

        If her suspicion was correct, she wasn't investigating an ordinary murder. She was investigating the death of a monster. And the person who killed him was still out there.

        Elena looked toward the dark forest.

        Somewhere beyond those trees was a killer.

        The problem was that, for the first time in her career, she wasn't sure whether bringing him to justice was the right thing to do.
